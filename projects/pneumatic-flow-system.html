Pneumatic Flow Control System

Design and development of a four-channel pneumatic liquid-delivery system for microfluidic research, created to address the flow oscillations observed with mechanically driven syringe pumps.

Fluid Mechanics Microfluidics Pneumatics Onshape 3D Printing Prototyping

Second-generation pneumatic flow control system.

01 · Engineering Problem

Creating stable flow for microfluidic experiments

Graduate researchers conducting microfluidic experiments required stable liquid flow through small SLA-printed microchannels. Mechanically driven syringe pumps introduced periodic flow oscillations that caused the interface between fluid streams to move during experiments.

The objective of this project was to develop an alternative fluid-delivery system using pneumatic pressure as the driving force. The system needed to support independently adjustable liquid streams while reducing the mechanically induced oscillations observed with the existing syringe-pump setup.

02 · System Architecture

Four independently controlled pneumatic channels

A small air compressor supplied a primary pressure regulator. The regulated air was routed through a manifold that divided the pneumatic supply into four independently controlled channels.

Each channel incorporated a pressure gauge, toggle valve and needle valve. The controlled pneumatic supply was then routed to liquid reservoirs, where air pressure provided the driving force necessary to deliver liquid to the microfluidic device.

03 · Proof of Concept

Prototype 1 — Open pegboard layout

The first prototype used an open pegboard arrangement to establish component placement, assemble the pneumatic architecture and evaluate the operating concept before developing a more integrated system.

This configuration allowed the regulator, manifold, toggle valves, needle valves, reservoirs and tubing to remain accessible while the system was being assembled and tested.

First prototype used to develop and test the pneumatic architecture.

04 · Design Iteration

Prototype 2 — Integrated enclosure

After establishing the basic operating concept, the system was redesigned as an integrated enclosure. I developed the complete enclosure and component layout in Onshape, including mounting locations, openings, pass-throughs and internal supports for the pneumatic and fluid-handling hardware.

05 · Design for Manufacturing

Selecting the appropriate fabrication method

Although the enclosure was designed digitally as an integrated structure, the available FDM printer did not have sufficient build volume to manufacture the enclosure as a single piece.

Dividing the enclosure into multiple printed sections would have introduced additional joints and unnecessary structural complexity. Instead, I fabricated the primary enclosure from wood while retaining additive manufacturing for specialized mounting components where 3D printing provided a clear advantage.

06 · CAD & Additive Manufacturing

Custom component mounting

Off-the-shelf pneumatic hardware required custom mounting solutions for integration into the enclosure. I modeled purpose-built risers and supports in Onshape and manufactured them using FDM 3D printing.

07 · CAD Development

Component integration and packaging

Additional CAD development was used to evaluate component placement, mounting geometry and packaging before fabrication. This allowed the physical arrangement of the system to be considered before committing to the final enclosure.

08 · Testing & Validation

Evaluating flow stability

Average liquid flow rate was evaluated gravimetrically by collecting the effluent over a measured time interval and determining the corresponding mass flow.

Dynamic behavior was evaluated visually using two differently colored water streams inside the microfluidic channel. With the mechanical syringe-pump setup, the interface between the streams visibly moved back and forth as the flow oscillated.

Under pneumatic operation, the interface remained visually stable. Adjusting the relative pressure supplied to each inlet also allowed the interface position to be shifted within the channel.

Measurement limitation: Instantaneous flow was not instrumented with a flow meter. The reduction in oscillation was therefore evaluated qualitatively through observation of the fluid interface rather than reported as a quantitative pulsation measurement.

Food-colored water was used to visualize the interface between independently controlled liquid streams.

09 · Experimental Integration

Adapting the microfluidic hardware

The SLA-printed microfluidic device originated from an existing research design and was not originally designed by me. I modified the inlet geometry to improve tubing fit and sealing for integration with the pneumatic delivery system.

This allowed the existing research hardware to interface more reliably with the newly developed fluid-delivery system without representing the underlying microfluidic device as an original design.

Project Outcome

From research limitation to engineered prototype

The project produced a pneumatically driven, independently adjustable fluid-delivery platform that provided a visibly stable fluid interface during microfluidic testing.

More broadly, the project required translating a research limitation into an engineering system through fluid mechanics, pneumatic control, CAD, prototyping, fabrication, experimental testing and iterative design.
